What Is Generative AI? A Simple Beginner's Guide

Generative AI describes systems that can create new text, images, audio, code and other content from instructions. It is becoming part of everyday work, education and creativity.

A generative system receives an instruction, processes it using patterns learned during training, and produces an output that fits the request. Different models are designed for different tasks.

What people use it for

Common uses include brainstorming, explaining difficult subjects, drafting outlines, summarizing information supplied by the user, and helping with creative work.

An answer that sounds confident is not automatically correct. Users should check important information and avoid treating an AI system as an unquestionable authority.

How to Recognize a Fake Website Before You Enter Information

Fake websites can imitate banks, social networks, shopping services and other trusted brands. Learning a few warning signs can reduce the risk of losing an account or sensitive information.

Read the full domain

A familiar brand name inside a strange or unrelated domain does not prove that the website is official. Read the complete address carefully.

Do not trust urgency

Scammers often create pressure by claiming that an account will be closed or a reward will disappear immediately. Verify the claim through the service's official app or website.

Remember that HTTPS is not proof of legitimacy

HTTPS encrypts the connection but does not guarantee that the website itself is legitimate. Always check the domain and how you reached the page.

Open important services directly

Stop when something feels wrong

How to Stay Safe From Online Scams

Many online scams rely on speed, fear or excitement. The safest habit is to slow down and verify unexpected requests before clicking, paying or sharing information.

Pause before acting

Unexpected urgency is a common warning sign. You do not have to respond immediately just because a message says you must.

Check the sender and destination

Look carefully at the full sender address, phone number, account name and website address. Small differences can matter.

Never share verification codes

One-time verification codes can approve logins or transactions. Do not give them to unexpected callers or messages.

Be careful with payment requests

What Is Two-Factor Authentication and Why Does It Matter?

Two-factor authentication, often called 2FA, adds another verification step to an account. It can make unauthorized access much harder when a password is exposed.

Why 2FA helps

A password is only one layer of protection. A second factor can be an authenticator app, security key, device approval or another method supported by the service.

Common second factors

Enable two-step verification on important accounts whenever it is available, especially email and accounts that can reset other passwords.

Where to enable it

Keep backup codes private. Store them somewhere secure and never publish or send them to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

How to Create Strong Passwords You Can Actually Manage

Strong passwords are easier to maintain when every important service has a different password. A password manager can reduce the burden of remembering them all.

Use unique passwords

A password that is reused across multiple websites creates a chain reaction if one service is breached. Use a different password for important accounts.

Prefer long passwords

Longer passwords are generally easier to make strong than short, complicated patterns. Avoid names, birthdays and other information that can be guessed.

Avoid predictable personal information

The password manager itself becomes important, so protect its main account carefully and use the security features it provides.
